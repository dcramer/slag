# art3d: the cast's drawn kinds

The production pipeline for the cast: one kind's entry (`docs/characters/<kind>.md`) in, one kind the game draws out. Every rendered kind is a **flat kind**: designed once at game size, worn in an edit, drawn in a dozen poses by the image model with the design as its reference, and each drawing traced flat into the game's own shapes (docs/CHARACTERS.md → Rendered kinds). The game ships only the generated `src/render/cast/sprites/<kind>/frames.ts`; a kind with frames is always drawn from them. Images are free up to the account's 100 a month (the CLI prints what's left), then 2 Studio credits each. Drawn three poses to an image, a kind that passes its first blind read takes 14 to 18 images; each failed read costs a redraw, and a kind that fails several (the robot, the king, the rapper's 77) can take three or four times that. Agents share the allowance, so kinds drawn side by side run it out together. The owner approved paid credits for the cast (2026-11-04).

The folder's name is history: kinds were rendered from 3D models until 2026-11-03, and that route is retired ([3D.md](3D.md)). Its tracer (`page/trace.ts`) is this route's too.

One agent per kind, in a worktree of its own: [CHECKLIST.md](CHECKLIST.md) is the run sheet and the done list. This README is the how.

```bash
node tools/art3d/style.ts design KIND           # the design prompt (`worn KIND` the wear edit's); `build.ts pose KIND NAME` a pose's
node /home/dcramer/src/terra/tools/tripo/tripo.ts image "PROMPT" --aspect 1:1 --count 4 -o design.png
node /home/dcramer/src/terra/tools/tripo/tripo.ts image "PROMPT" --ref art/sprites/KIND/design.png --aspect 16:9 --count 2 -o sheetA.png
pnpm exec tsx tools/art3d/flat/build.ts cut KIND sheetA.png walk-1 walk-2 idle2 --design   # a sheet of poses cut into art/sprites/KIND/flat/
python3 -I tools/art3d/flat/swatch.py art/sprites/KIND/design.png shots/KIND-swatch.png   # the design's colours, for map.families
pnpm exec tsx tools/art3d/flat/build.ts KIND    # trace every pose into src/render/cast/sprites/KIND/frames.ts
FLAT_DEBUG=idle,wall pnpm exec tsx tools/art3d/flat/build.ts KIND   # those poses' eye specks and the eyes found, to tune them; writes nothing
pnpm vitest run tests/sprite-cast.test.ts       # every kind held to the cast's box, eyes and variety
pnpm shot variety --kinds KIND [--theme mine]   # twelve looks, eight of it at play scale and 3×, the cast lineup
pnpm shot sprites --kind KIND                   # the run cycle, the six-rolls lobby, the draw cost, the blind silhouette sheet
```

`tripo.ts` reads terra's `.env`, so `node` it from anywhere. Run it bare, by its full path, with the prompt pasted in as one literal string: the user settings allow exactly `node /home/dcramer/src/terra/tools/tripo/tripo.ts image *` and `… balance`, and a `$tripo` variable or a `$(…)` prompt is neither that rule nor something the worktree guard lets through. The build needs python3 with Pillow. Sources under `art/` are in Git LFS (`.gitattributes`); builds and tests never read them, only `frames.ts`. Role maps land in `.cache/flat/KIND/` (a `*.png` beside each, to check a pose's roles).

**Tripo's refusals.** `403: Your project has been banned due to violation of our policy` is a filter on that prompt, usually for something that reads as a copyrighted character, not a ban on the account (`… balance` still answers, and other prompts go through). Don't resend it: take out the franchise's names and its most trademark detail, describe the shape instead, and lean on the twist. Several agents drawing at once can get each other's images back under their own job (the top-hat girl's first designs were the creeper's), so look at what came back before you pick from it. Give each agent a scratch folder of its own, too: two sharing one overwrote each other's scripts mid-run.

## 1. Design, at game size

The `design` prompt, `--aspect 1:1 --count 4`, no reference. `DESIGN` asks for one figure that reads as a 24 px sprite: one silhouette idea, about three big shapes, four flat colours plus ink, the prop, the eyes. Pick the one with the clearest silhouette and the twist as shapes, not detail.

- **The original redrawn.** For a famous design the image model draws the original whatever the brief says (the hedgehog came back with the joined eye shape and long legs twice). Two ways out: the kind's approved concept as the reference, with the brief's twist named again (`--ref art/sprites/KIND/concept.png`; the hedgehog's two eyes and heavy brows came back that way), or the twist pushed in the wear edit (the plumber's welding goggles). Never ship one that reads as the original redrawn (docs/CHARACTERS.md → Roster).
- **Put the signature prop first** in the brief: the image model ignores most of a prompt past its first ~1,800 characters.
- **Side on** reads better at play scale than the three-quarter view `DESIGN` asks for, for any kind whose read is a profile (a snout, a shell, a jaw, a box on legs, four legs): in three-quarter the parts sit in one round mass (the king, the sneak, the horse, the dragon, the hopper, the rapper). Say so in the brief.
- **Read the design before the poses.** Black out the picked design (every pixel darker than the background's white) at about 22 px tall and give it, beside a few cast silhouettes, to one or two fresh readers (CHECKLIST.md's prompt). A design that fails there fails the sheet, and a new design costs a few images, not a dozen.

## 2. The wear, as an edit

The `worn` prompt, `--ref design.png --aspect 1:1 --count 3`. Asked for in the design prompt, the damage is dropped; as an edit of the picked design it lands and the design holds. Two to four big, worn shape changes (bent, cracked, taped, torn, patched, a sole flapping), never dirt or a rough line (Decision 9). Keep it off the face: a tape cross beside an eye reads as a third eye at play size. Save the pick as `art/sprites/KIND/design.png`; it is the `idle` pose.

## 3. Poses

Each pose's prompt is `posePrompt` (`flat/kinds.ts`) on the kind's `poses`, with the design as the reference: the image model copies a reference closely, which is what holds the character from pose to pose. Keep the best of each as `art/sprites/KIND/flat/NAME.png`, and every prompt in `art/sprites/KIND/design.prompt.txt`. The usual set: `idle2`, `walk-1`, `walk-2`, `run-1`, `run-2`, `air-1` (rising), `air-2` (falling), `wall`, `cheer`, `cheer2`, `death`.

- **`far`** for a pose far from the design's stance (lying knocked out, side on to a wall): it lets go of the view, or the model hands back the design's stance.
- **Reject** a drawing that lost a prop or the cap (the elf's falls threw his cap off), grew limbs (the dot-eater sprouted legs; a stretched leap drew the hopper a third trainer), changed a colour (a black plunger cup), or reads as a different expression than the clip (shut eyes are fine anywhere but the idle; the face kit has none to ride). One turned to the left is mirrored, not redrawn (the engineer's).
- **Every pose keeps the read.** End each pose prompt with the shapes the silhouette needs in every frame (the king's `KEEP`): arms flung up over the head read as the ape whatever the body, and a prop raised or held across the chest vanishes into the outline (the hunter's cannon, the robot's can), so it is held out level, clear of the body.
- **A drawn wall line** beside a wall slide is whitened out of the drawing before tracing: the column dark most of the way down, and only what's darker than the figure's fills (the plumber's and the dot-eater's).
- **Three poses to an image**, the default: a 16:9 image with two or three poses side by side, cut apart along each figure's own outline onto its own square, costs a third of drawing each alone and holds the character better across them. Two of each sheet; keep the best figure of each pose from either.
- **Clean the sheet first.** The model sometimes draws a grey floor shadow under the figures or an off-white background; both trace as part of the figure. Paint them white before cutting: the grey reachable from the border without crossing the ink.
- **Cutting a sheet:** `build.ts cut KIND SHEET NAME…` names the figures left to right (`skip` drops one) and writes each to `art/sprites/KIND/flat/NAME.png`, padded onto a white square (`--pad`, 1.25 of its longer side). A figure is everything one outline closes in, its white body, glints and gaps included (background is what's lighter than `--white`, 228, flooded in from the border). The biggest pieces, one a name, are the figures; every other big piece (a floating glove or shoe) joins them nearest pair first by outline, so a shoe can join by way of its glove, and a speck within reach goes to the nearest figure; and figures that touch are split along the cheapest seam between them, through white or ink, never a fill. Each keeps the scale it was drawn at, and `body` sizes every pose alike. `--design` scales every figure of the sheet by one factor, the one that draws them as big as the design (by area), so the ink is as thick as the design's and `ink` and `fringe` read every drawing alike; `--scale K` sets the factor (the horse's 1.44). Use `--design` unless a sheet's figures were drawn at different sizes on purpose.
- **Floating parts** (the limbless hero's gloves and shoes): the model draws the gap about a world px wide, which the pen closes at play size. Slide each piece clear before tracing, about 2 world px of background all round (`art/sprites/limbless/spread.py`, sources in `drawn/`), and make them `loose` so `body` sizes the rest.
- **When the model won't change the pose** (the miner's stiff toy came back in the design's stance every time), cut the poses from the design: limbs turned about their joints, the figure tipped or laid flat (`art/sprites/miner/puppet.py`). A part the model multiplies when asked to move it (the jelly's fangs) is posed the same way. A drawing already in the set that does the job is reused instead (below).
- **Reuse** a drawing where a pose adds nothing, or where its own drawing can't take the face: a ball slides down a wall in its fall (the hopper's wall drawing tipped his eyes off level), the elf's one jump drawing is his whole air clip, the hedgehog's jump is two spin-ball drawings looping.

## 4. The map

How `rolemap.py` reads the drawings, in the kind's `map` (`flat/kinds/<kind>.ts`):

- **`families`**: every colour of the design, shades and highlights included, each named for its role. `swatch.py` prints them and draws a sheet beside the design. A shade folds into its role: the drawing's cel shadow is gone, the figure is flat. A shade that carries a shape (the plumber's cap shadow and plunger cup) is a role of its own drawn as a dark of the costume (`on`, `tone`), so it follows the player's colour.
- **`line`** is the ink's role. Thin ink is the line and goes; ink thicker than `ink` px (default 9) stays a fill (the ear tips, the mouth). A thin dark prop (the elf's poker) gets a role of its own, or a lower `ink`. `ink` must be odd (a filter size: the build refuses an even one), and so must `fringe`.
- **`thin` and `fringe`**: the antialiased dots of a colour along the ink. Every colour role goes in `thin`, with `fringe: 5`, or the outline is speckled.
- **`white` glints** inside the eyes go to the ink's role, so the eyes trace whole.
- **`feet`** are what the figure stands on (its lowest pixels and its centre); **`loose`** roles are left out of its size (a tail, a carried poker). `body` sets every pose to one size: `pnpm exec tsx tools/art3d/flat/build.ts body KIND HEIGHT` prints the one that draws the design HEIGHT world px tall (the shipped kind's height, or about 20 to 22).
- **`min`** per role: the smallest part worth a shape. Raise it to drop slivers (a seam's highlight, the scraps of a tape cross's ink).
- **`smooth`**: the tracer's corner-cutting passes (default 2). Each pass takes a quarter off both edges at every corner, so a box traces round: the miner's cube head is `smooth: 0`. It also rounds a small point into a leaf: the floating diamond's first blind read was "a Pikmin" until `smooth: 0` kept its points.
- **`stroke`**: the shortest interior line kept (default 1.4 world px; 2.4 for a detailed drawing, or its gloves and goggles fill with flecks). A long line across a ball (the puffball's seam) reads as a mouth at play size: `stroke: 99`, none.
- **`white`**: with `holes`, how light a hole's pixel may be and still be the background (the least of r, g and b, default 228). The ink between a gap and a white body went half to a white rim inside the hole; lower (the cuptoon's 150) takes the hole's antialiased rim with it and gives that ink to the figure, as the outline's goes outside. A patch is a hole only if most of it is lighter than 228, so a white body is never one.
- **`holes`**: the drawing's background closed in by the figure (the gap between an arm and the body, between the legs, a handle's eye), each patch over this many world px², cut through the frame so the level shows (the engineer's 0.8). Traced white, a gap shows as a white blob; folded into a colour, as a blotch. Off by default, since a white body (the goose) is closed in too, and set above a glint's or a tooth's size. Eye whites closed in by the outline are holes too: tint them a step off white in the drawings (the hopper's `whites.py`) and `holes` leaves them.
- **`cut`**: a drawn hole that is the read becomes a real one. The dot-eater's open mouth is a dark patch in the drawing; filled, its silhouette is a plain circle and the blind read failed. `cut: { role: 'dark', area: 2 }` takes out every dark patch over 2 world px² open to the outside.
- **A dark that reads as the wrong thing** (a shadow between the goose's legs, a speck at play size) goes to a role of its own with `recolour`; a dark under the face reads as a frowning mouth, so draw a belt or cord there in its own colour.

## 5. Roles, face, variety, frames

- **Roles** (Decisions 4 in docs/CHARACTERS.md): the costume, the region a stranger names the colour by and usually the biggest, is `slot: 'player'`. Every other coloured part is a tone of it, `on: <its index>, tone: ±N` (about 20 to 40 apart, so the parts stay separate shapes), and a tone may sit on a tone: they resolve in index order, so the one it sits on comes first, or is the player's. Wear marks are tones of the surface they sit on (`on`, ±8 to ±18). Everything else is a neutral `color`: skin, eyes, hair, metal, wood, bone, cloth greys, the ink. Palette `alts` are neutrals only. A dark tone that is fine on a light player colour sinks into the outline on a navy or purple one (the king's mane at −36): a shape that must stay a shape on every colour is a light tone. `tests/sprite-cast.test.ts` checks that only the costume and its tones change with the player; that the rest are neutral is yours to keep.
- **`face`**: the face kit draws the kind's own eye where the drawing's were, so it acts (docs/CHARACTERS.md → Rendered kinds → The cast's face). `eye` is the homage's eye from the kit's registry (`EYES` in `src/render/cast/sprites/face.ts`; `EyeStyle` in `types.ts`): `round` (the default), `tall`, `dot`, `pill`, `profile`, `scowl`, `cat`, `heavy`, `anime`, `square`, `glow`, `bulb`, `googly`, `goggle`, `socket`, `cutout` (the drawing's own dark eyes, a hole or solid ink, which the kit only shuts with `face.lid` and acts round: the creeper's) or `printed` (pale shut eyes printed on a sleep mask worn over hers, which act anyway: the android's); a new homage's eye is a new entry there, never a branch elsewhere. `r` is the near eye's size in world px, fitted to the drawing's eye and the head: an eye reads at play scale from about 0.7, and two eyes the drawing sets close must not overlap at it unless the homage's touch (the dino's). `iris` shows only on a pupil big enough to carry it (about 0.45 world px), so a small eye's is ink whatever it says. `far` is a side-on head's far eye: a face found with `count: 1` and a `pair` gets `'sliver'` (tucked behind the near one) unless it says `'none'` (the horse, the dragon, the king, the puppy, whose drawings show one eye); never two full eyes side by side, which `tests/sprite-cast.test.ts` holds. A three-quarter face whose drawings show both eyes apart is found as a pair, with `far: 'turned'`: the far eye where it's drawn, foreshortened, so two close eyes on a narrow face don't touch and read as goggles (the slayer's). `mouth` is the kit's mouth's offset from the near eye, if it draws one, and `mark` the least width of shut eyes and cheer arcs. The kit clips the eyes to the figure's outline. Rebuilt after a face change, a kind's frames change only in `face` and the eye sizes. `pnpm shot cast --only faces` shows every kind's face in every mood, close up and at play scale.
- **`eyes`**: the face kit replaces the drawing's eyes, specks at play size. The eyes are the pair of `role` specks `area` big and `gap` apart, in the figure's top `band`, never on its outline (narrow `band` and cap `area` to keep them out of a dark open mouth or throat just below: the king's); `count: 1` takes the biggest, for one eye (the dot-eater) or a face turned nearly side on, with `pair` setting the far eye from the near one (the elf's sits on his outline, the hedgehog's is lost in its white). `specks` are what goes from the face box: the drawing's eyes, nose and mouth. Keep `face.r` small enough that two kit eyes don't overlap where the drawing's sit close. `FLAT_DEBUG=<pose>,…` traces only those poses and prints their specks with their area, band, distance to the edge and pixel in the role map (`.cache/flat/KIND/<pose>.png`), and the eyes found; it writes nothing. A dark mouth or collar just under the eyes is often bigger than an eye: stop `band` above it and cap `area` under it (the puppy's, the mercenary's). `edge` lowers the least distance from the outline, for a far eye hard by the cheek's line (the slayer's). `in` takes only a speck with a third of its rim on that role, a pupil in its white, when other darks are as big (the dragon's wing panels); put the white's role in `specks` too, or its rim shows round the kit's eye. A drawing that hides its eyes (the puppy's design, under his beanie) sets the near one by hand, `at` per pose in fractions of the figure's box. A small face's eyes (a few map pixels across) don't survive the tracer's smoothing: `from: 'map'` finds them in the role map (the engineer's goggle lenses). Eyes drawn tucked under a brim put the kit's shut eyes and cheer arcs on the brim's ink: `drop` moves the kit's eyes down after the drawn face is painted over, and `face.mark` sets the least width of those marks, so a small eye's aren't a hairline; a clip's `shift` moves them on that clip alone, for a head tilted in one pose. Pupils drawn as an ink ring round a white glint, or a dot narrower than `ink`, are taken for line and go: `glint` (world px², the biggest glint) fills each patch the ink alone closes in within the `band`, then keeps every round lone speck of ink there up to `area`'s top as a fill (the horse's drawings, unpainted, put the face where its painted pupils did in 7 of the 10 open-eyed poses with `glint: 0.25`, 3 without). The face box's specks are painted over whole in the role map before the pose is traced again, so the face traces with no notch. Specks under 0.6 px² are drawn with no pen, so a glint doesn't get a pupil's ring. `recolour` takes several rules.
- **`wear`**: scuffs and a scorch, drawn as slanted streaks (a round blob reads as a spot), in tones of the surface they're `on`, at spots that keep their place in its box from pose to pose, only on bare surface below the `chin`. "No room for" in the build's output means a mark didn't fit a pose: make it smaller. On a smooth ball one quiet mark each (tones ±8 to ±18): a ring of blobs reads as a rash.
- **`accessories`**: a strip of tape or a plaster, on the crown or across the cheek, from the near eye. `offset` moves one off its place: on a small face the cheek's lands on the jaw, on the puffball under its blush. Not on a ball's crown: a cross there reads as a bow, and a ball with a bow as another character.
- **Colourways** must not land on another role's colour: drawn flat, the plumber's brown-canvas overalls were his tan gloves, and the colour block went.
- **Clips**: `frames` and timing (`fps`, `stride`, `vy`, `holds`). `eyes: false` for a pose whose eyes are shut. `centre: 'box'` and `scale` for a pose too wide for the cast's box (the lying death, the hedgehog's and the elf's wall slides, the dot-eater's pancake).

## 6. Check

Register the kind in `LOADERS` (`src/render/cast/sprites/index.ts`). `tests/sprite-cast.test.ts` loads every kind there: each clip in both facings, inside the shared box pen and all (every slot option, the boldest gait), at least 18 px tall, every eye on its head and figure at the kit's size, the variety layers present and spread over the seeds, no wear on the face. Then look, at play scale and 3×, on a light theme and a dark one, beside the cast (`pnpm shot variety`), and the run cycle and lobby (`pnpm shot sprites`). The blind read is in the checklist.

A test that draws the hand-drawn bodies calls `useSprites(false)` in a `beforeAll`: a draw starts fetching its kind's frames, and once that import lands mid-file the runner is drawn from them instead (`tests/ink-character.test.ts`).

## How it draws

- **Tracing** (`flat/build.ts`): each drawing's role map, at 12 px per world px, is cut into parts and traced by `page/trace.ts` with no depth steps (a drawing is flat); interior lines are thinned to their centres and walked into strokes. The frame's feet sit on the floor with the pen just under it; facing left is the mirror.
- **Drawing** (`src/render/cast/sprites/`): `draw.ts` draws a flat set's silhouette in one neutral ink (so the gaps between parts never show the costume's colour, or a dark of it) and its parts filled flat under a light pen (`flat`), the interior lines in that ink, no hard shadow of its own (its offset mottled thin limbs), and the face from `face.ts`; `index.ts` dresses it for a character (the costume's colour, the palette, the rolled accessories and wear, the gait's lift and lean) and keeps each drawn frame as a bitmap; `play.ts` picks the clip and frame from the pose.
- **Light**: every rendered figure marks its shape in the light map's alpha (`Renderer.markFigures`), and the cel pass lights it evenly, with no light band across it (`figAmbient` in `cel-shader.ts`).
