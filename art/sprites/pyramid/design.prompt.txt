How design.png and the pose drawings were made (2026-11-05). Every image was 2 credits (no free images left). The drafts are in drafts/, the pose sheets in sheets/, cut into flat/.

1. Design, round 1 (`node tools/art3d/style.ts design pyramid` as the brief first stood: "one huge metal pyramid, a sharp clean triangular wedge much wider than his shoulders, its flat base resting on his shoulders and its long point sticking far out to the right"; 4 drawn, drafts/r1-design-1..4). All four drew a tall upright pyramid on his head, a pointed hat more than a helmet, and the slicer small and in front.

2. Round 2, the head asked for as a wedge, not a hat ("its back edge rises straight up from his back shoulder, and its top edge slopes down to one long sharp point sticking straight out sideways to the right, level with his chin"; 4 drawn, drafts/r2-design-1..4): the wedge came, but thin, and the slicer stayed in front of him (or became a kitchen knife). Two edits of r2-design-4: the slicer asked for behind him (drafts/slicer-1..3: it came back across his legs, bigger), then the wedge fattened into one solid triangle (drafts/fat-1..3).
   Design read, fat-1 and fat-2 as black silhouettes 24 px tall beside the merc, king, engineer, stinger, warrior, elf and plumber designs, two fresh readers: "black mage / wizard (medium). Tall pointed hat with a wide brim that sweeps to the right, a bulky robe flaring at the base, and no visible face or limbs"; "black mage / wizard (medium): a tall pointed hat with the tip leaning up-left, a brim sticking out right, and a robe that flares wide at the bottom with no visible legs." The slicer across his legs flared his base into a robe, and the helmet's ledge read as a brim: the mage's read.

3. Round 3, legs apart and the slicer dragged behind (4 drawn, drafts/r3-design-1..4; one edit of r3-design-4 to make the slicer three times as big, drafts/big-1..3, came back hardly bigger).

4. Round 4, the dragged slicer first in the brief and the wedge as a right-angled triangle (the brief in tools/art3d/style.ts now; 4 drawn, drafts/r4-design-1..4). r4-design-1 kept: a rust wedge with its point out past his front shoulder, his legs apart, the slicer slanting from his fist to the floor behind him. r4-design-3 drew the helmet grey; r4-design-2 and 4 a knight's pot helmet inside a steel triangle.
   Design read, r4-design-1 and r4-design-3 beside the stinger, king, merc, elf, engineer and warrior designs, two fresh readers: "Pyramid Head (Silent Hill), medium: a tall triangular helmet with the peak off-center, a heavy body and a long blade dragged on the ground at lower left. A witch or black mage in a pointed hat with a broom or staff is the other possible reading"; "Pyramid Head (Silent Hill), high. A tall triangular helmet, a hulking body, a huge blade dragged low on the left." Each paired only the two candidates.

5. The twist and the wear, as one edit (--ref drafts/r4-design-1.png, 3 drawn; drafts/worn-1 kept as design.png: the rivets, the dent, the pig, the torn hem and the bitten slicer, and the least rust mottling):
The same character exactly as in the reference image: the same pose, design, proportions, colours, line and flat white background. Change only this, and make each change big and obvious: the helmet is an old rusty roof-vent cowl: a row of big round rivets along its bottom edge and a big dent knocked into its sloping top face; a big cartoon pig face in pink (#e8a0a8), round with a snout and two ears, is printed on the front of the apron; the apron hem is torn into a ragged notch at one side; a big chip is knocked out of the cheese slicer blade edge. The helmet's wedge shape, its point and the slot stay exactly as they are. Nothing else changes, no dirt, no texture.

6. Poses, three to a 16:9 image with design.png as the reference, each drawn twice, cut with `build.ts cut pyramid SHEET … --design`. Each prompt is the template below with the kind's KEEP (tools/art3d/flat/kinds/pyramid.ts) and three poses (the poses' words are each pose's in that file):
The same character exactly as in the reference image: the same design, proportions, colours, wear and tear, line and flat white background, in the same three-quarter view facing right. Draw him three times side by side in one row: three separate small figures of the same size, each alone in its own third of the picture, with very wide white space between them, none touching or overlapping, each in its own pose, KEEP. Left: … Middle: … Right: … Nothing else changes: no ground, no shadow on the ground, no motion lines, no text, no other figures.
   - sheetA (two plods, the helmet scratch): A-2 -> flat/walk-1, walk-2, idle2 (A-1's slicers reached into the next figure).
   - sheetB (two lurches, a jump): B-1 -> flat/run-1, run-2; B-2 -> flat/air-1 (B-1's jump held the slicer up in front of him).
   - sheetC (falling, a fist pump, a slow wave): C-1 -> flat/air-2, cheer, cheer2 (C-2's fist pump swung the slicer in front of him).
   - sheetD, the far template, two figures ("The same character as in the reference image … drawn at the same size", the wall slide side on and knocked out on his back): D-1 -> flat/wall, death, its drawn wall line whitened first (sheetD-1-clean.png). D-2's wall slide faced left.

7. Blind read, round 1 (r1-flat/, the first design, drafts/design-r1.png), two fresh readers on 33: "Black Mage from Final Fantasy (medium-low). 8. The long wedge and the bigger slicer, an edit of design-r1 (3 drawn, drafts/wedge-1..3; wedge-2 kept: the longest point and the biggest paddle; it dropped the slot and the slicer's bites):
The same character exactly as in the reference image: the same pose, apron, pig, arms, legs, boots, colours, rust, rivets, line and flat white background. Change only the helmet and the cheese slicer, and make each change big and obvious: the helmet is lower and much longer, a long flat wedge twice as wide as it is tall: its back edge short and upright above his back shoulder, its bottom edge flat and level on his shoulders, and its sharp point sticking far out to the right, as far past his front shoulder as his body is wide, with white background above and below the point; no brim, no ledge, no rim along its bottom. The cheese slicer is twice as long, its paddle blade twice as big, slanting from his fist far out behind him to the left down to the ground. Nothing else changes, no dirt, no texture.
   Then an edit of wedge-2 asking the wedge to overhang both shoulders like a roof (3 drawn, drafts/roof-1..3): all three came back a symmetric paddy-field hat, none kept.
   handle.py slides wedge-2's paddle 60 px up its own handle (design.png): trailing that far, the idle reached past the cast's box behind the hitbox.
