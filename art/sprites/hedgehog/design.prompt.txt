How design.png and the pose drawings were made (the tin wind-up toy, 2026-11-06). The owner found the scrapyard runner (scrapyard/: its design and flat/ poses) the original redrawn: its twist, a chipped tooth, a neckerchief and steel toecaps, shrank to specks. This round changes the outline and a big colour block: a wind-up key out of his hip. Every image was 2 credits; drafts are in tin/.

1. Tin toy, an edit of scrapyard/design.png (2 drawn; tin/tin-1.png kept: two separate eyes, a grey eye plate; the other drew the original's joined eyes):
The same character as in the reference image: the same big head, face shape, swept-back quills, pose, proportions, three-quarter view facing right, thin dark line and flat white background. Rebuild him as an old pressed-tin wind-up toy, and make each change big and obvious. A big wind-up key sticks straight out of the small of his back below the quills: a thick grey steel stem and a big flat figure-eight bow with two round holes in it, the bow as tall as his body, standing well clear of him with white background all round it. His quills are four flat strips of blue-painted tin, each with a folded edge, swept straight back. His head and body are blue-painted tin halves joined by a raised seam that runs round his outline, with a row of big round rivets along it. His muzzle and belly are flat peach-painted tin plates. Two separate round painted eyes with green irises, and a cocky one-sided painted grin. Simple grey tin mitten hands. Huge chunky boxy red tin shoes with a thick riveted grey sole. No neckerchief, no gloves. Flat colours, no shine, no gradients, no texture.

2. From tin-1 (2 drawn; tin/tinb-1.png kept: the pale folded quill edges and boxy shoes came, the key did not grow):
The same tin wind-up toy hedgehog as in the reference image: ... Change only this ... The wind-up key is twice as big and sticks much further out behind him ... Each of his four quills is a flat bent strip of blue tin with a wide pale-blue folded edge along its top. His two eyes are separate round white eyes with green irises, blue tin between them, no grey plate. His arms and mitten hands are peach-painted tin, not grey. His shoes are huge boxy red tin blocks with square toes and a thick grey riveted sole. ...
Traced black at 22 px, the drawn key sat inside the quills' outline: so it was redrawn by hand bigger and further out (scratch bigkey.py, tin/keyed-2.png).

3. The wear, from keyed-2 (2 drawn; tin/worn-1.png kept, the other lost its colours):
The same tin wind-up toy hedgehog exactly as in the reference image: the same pose, big head, face, cocky grin, swept-back tin quills with pale folded edges, seams, the big grey wind-up key sticking straight out of his back with its huge figure-eight bow standing well clear of him, the boxy red tin shoes, colours, proportions, three-quarter view facing right, line and flat white background. Redraw it all cleanly in one consistent thin hand-inked line, and make these worn changes big and obvious: a big dent is pushed into the top of his tin head; the lowest quill strip is bent down at a sharp kink; the front shoe's grey sole has come loose and gapes open at the toe. Fewer, bigger rivets: a few big round rivets along each seam. Nothing else changes, no dirt, no texture, no shine.
Design reads (one fresh reader each, black at 22 px beside cast designs): worn-1, "unsure. Small pointed ears or horns on top, a large round object with holes held on the left (a gauntlet or bomb?), short legs" (the key's bow against the quills); a colour read of it, "Sonic: (b) a knock-off with a twist. He has a big grey wind-up key on his back, so he reads as a clockwork toy". So the key was moved down to the hip by hand, clear of the quills (scratch movekey.py, tin/low-1.png).

4. Longer quills, from low-1 (2 drawn; the first kept as design.png, the second's quills thin as wires):
