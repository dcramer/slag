# Modes and their reference games

Every playstyle mode (`src/game/modes.ts`) borrows its mechanics from a game that already plays well, and takes its targets from how that game plays. This page lists, per mode, the reference game, what we borrow from it, the attributes and measurements that define the mode, where the reference sits, and where we sit today. [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md) → Modes holds the rules as they stand; [DECISIONS.md](DECISIONS.md) logs the calls.

Reading the tables:

- **Reference** is the reference game's number, with its source under Sources. "Not documented" means the research found no source; an estimate is marked as one.
- **Ours** is measured with bots unless it's a tuning constant. The match numbers come from six four-bot matches per mode (seeds 1–6, Normal bots, default rules; bots pick and place in a few seconds, so a human round adds most of the pick and place timers, about 20 s).
- **Target** is what we aim for. It's the owner's call where it differs from the reference.

### How catch-up got to half: level design and mechanics

| | The lead-camera racer | Rising Slag today |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Track | handmade horizontal loops, everyone on the same line at about the same speed, with high and low routes (grapple ceilings over the ground) | a vertical shaft, generated per match: open air, ledges staggered like bricks with several lines up every height, a wall-jump chimney up one side, two gates across it a lap (until 2026-11-05: storeys with two ways up each) |
| Pace | running speed; a lap is a stretch of screens | climbing, about 5 rows a second; a 48-row lap is about 10 s |
| Overtaking | constant: shortcuts, boosts and stuns reorder the pack | about 3 lead changes a round with the gates (leader on top a second straight), from 1.5: the pack comes through a gate together |
| Where the leader is | ahead, on your line | above you; most of our items fire or swing sideways |
| What a hit costs | a stun: seconds of position | a bonk; on a perch, a storey, which is strong but only if you can reach the leader |
| Catch-up items | the Golden Hook swaps you with the one ahead; missiles, crates and freeze aim ahead | the Queue Jumper swaps you with the one above, the Upper Hand punches up through floors, the winged shell hunts the leader |

So the open shaft alone works against catch-up: a lead holds even between equals. Before the gates, four bots with the same hands and no items still keep the 15 s leader on top 62% of the time (512 seeds); a lead of under 3 rows at 15 s wins 41%, 6–8 rows 71%, 12 or more 86–88%. A longer leash, so rounds last longer after 15 s, is what moved it most.

Tried, in order:

1. **Items that work upward** (built: the Queue Jumper and the Upper Hand). In every hand they move the early leader's wins about 5 points: trading places with one rival or punching one down rarely reorders the pack.
2. **The squeeze from the first elimination** (built): as the reference does. It shortens rounds, so the leash opens long at GO (built) to keep them at 20–40 s.
3. **Bots that get what a box deals** (built): braking at a walk, their hop under a box turned back and forth every frame and never jumped. Box uses doubled; errands are now short (3 tiles), since a detour behind the leader is climb lost.
4. **A start line** (built): four bots in one pocket jammed for seconds.
5. No effect, or worse, at 512 seeds: twice the boxes, solid floors with holes every fourth row, narrower ledges and more wells, no chimney, a lead deal of only Bubble Wrap, bots holding the Queue Jumper for the swap into the lead, bots climbing under whoever is just above (more bumps), lanes near a bot's own column (70%: the pack stops crossing).

6. **Regroup gates** (built, the owner's pick over items that cost the leader seconds): a shutter across the whole shaft, wall to wall, that shuts and opens on the race clock (`src/gen/level/loop-gates.ts`). Whoever reaches it shut waits under it while the pack climbs up, and the slag's floor pace waits while the leader does (`Tide.hold`), so a pack under a shut gate isn't eaten for the leader's wait. Every gate runs one cycle, so a leader who slips through one as it shuts meets the next one shut. Measured with the gate right over its ledges, at 512 seeds (boxes, bare): one gate a lap at mid-height 57% and 56%; two, a third and two thirds up, 51% and 53%; three 54% and 55%; two shut 5 s 49% and 49% with rounds 2 s longer; one shut 3 s 63% and 58%, 5.5 s 55% and 57% with 12% of rounds over by 15 s; one with the slag never waiting 57% and 58%, with more rounds over early; the slag waiting for anyone under a shut gate, not just the leader, the same as the leader alone. Kept: two gates, 4 s shut and 2 s open. A gate right over its ledges meant a jump onto them met it shut, so a pack waited a ledge lower with two jumps to make in the 2 s; it now goes a row higher where the shaft has room (50, 47 and 54 from 51, 51 and 53, and lone Easy bots through the first lap 91% again, from 78%). About half the runners who go out go out under a shut gate the leader got through: the gate splits a pack as well as gathering it.

Next: nothing for catch-up itself. Whether a gate that splits the pack that often feels fair is a playtest question.

## Brawl: the party brawler (Duck Game), killing by knockback into the level as the crossover brawler does (Smash)

What we borrow: short rounds, last one standing, items from boxes knocked from below, a draw has no winner. What's ours: knockouts score more than surviving, as the crossover brawler's timed matches count KOs [Smash-rules]; nothing kills on its own (items knock, bodies shove, on the race's physics, bodies shoving harder), the level does: its hazards, the parts placed in it every three bouts (so the arena gets nastier through a match), the drop, and late in a round slag pouring in from below.

The arena is a classic one-screen level of its own (`builder: 'arena'`, src/gen/level/builders/arena.ts), built for knocking people off things, as the reference game's and the crossover brawler's stages are: tiers of ledges from islands over the pit up into the top third of the screen, cut into bands of columns with shafts between them clear from the top tier down to the pit, so a knock off nearly any ledge falls out of the arena rather than onto the ledge below: 14% of the stand cells the field reaches have no way to die within 3 tiles (a shaft, the open side, a hazard; `standSafety`, src/gen/level/measure.ts), from 52% when each tier was staggered like bricks against the one under it and a fall mostly landed on a ledge, and bots spend 25% of their time on the ground somewhere that safe, from 49% (owner call: too many safe places, too hard to push anyone off). Each tier's ledges step in a column at alternate ends, so every band climbs, and boards to jump up through and drop down; sides open to a knock: the ledges run off the map, where the race course walled both ends floor to ceiling. Its two ends mirror each other, tier for tier, with a lip two rows high at the outer edge of each spawn pocket so nobody walks off at GO (a post taller than a jump, as a race's spawn has, walled off about a third of the knocks off the map). Its shape varies by seed: full tiers, a pyramid with the high ground in the middle, or a bowl with it at the sides; and so do which tier the spawns stand on, the ledges and gaps, boards against floor, and which islands of the bottom tier stand on the bottom. No island stands under a spawn ledge, where it made a crawlspace a bot couldn't jump out of. One band either side is a hall, by the sides under a pyramid's drawn-in tiers or by the middle under a bowl's open one: a floor on the bottom tier 9 to 11 columns wide with no tier over it, a roof 9 rows up stepped a row down over its outer half (a low roof for a gas pocket, a tall one for a pendulum), a pier hanging 4 rows from it at its inner end (a wall face for a broken window's glass), and nothing over the roof but the sky. That's the room a set's pieces ask for that tiers a jump apart never leave: a press in its gantry, a forklift's or a cart's run, a ladle's pour, an axe's arc, a cave-in under a roof; the same for every set, so a factory arena stands 5.5 of its 6 pieces and a castle's 5.8 where they stood 2.6 and 3.8, the hall's floor and its roof's top each taking a machine. Lanes (gen/level/lanes.ts) keep out of a hall's columns (`Shape.open`). No tier leaves a void in the middle wider than its widest gap (a bowl's lower tiers get a board across it), and in low gravity the gaps and the rows between tiers stay a normal gravity's, so a space arena has as many places to stand as a farm's (about 95 stand cells the field reaches; 135 before the shafts, 72 before that). Nothing hangs in the top rows but a set's own canopy. It's built from the set's tower kit (its floor and dressing) and raises no landmark: the arena is the stage. It owes no part (`bare`), each end reaching the other bare (the pipeline proves both ways), everyone starting from its two ends (`ends`), deadly by default: six set pieces from the nastiest of its set's mix (`hazards: 6`, `danger: 0.9`), against 4 of the usual mix, and only what kills or knocks (`setPieceRoles: ['hazard', 'utility']`: no platform, lift or wheel to stand on), runners out before the slag in round 1 went from 0.54 a round to 0.75 (on the race course). Asking 8 made the sets with the most recipes (space, volcano, harbor) take 7 or 8 and the rest stay at 5 to 6, and cut rounds to 19.4 s, 19 of 48 in 20–40 s, so it stays 6. No gun's shot, bouncing off whatever else stands there, gets into either end (`shotsIntoSpawns`, for a level with `ends`), since the spawn shield ends 5 s after GO. Its boxes are mirrored too, a pair near the spawns' tier and one in the middle, each over a floor the field reaches with room on top for what it pops; where a set piece took a mirror or the middle, the nearest spot to the middle, so every arena hangs three or more. No set piece reaches through its ledges or boards (`throughFaults`, as for every set piece), and every board has two rows of air over it. Bots press the fight, as the reference game's players rush the first weapon and each other. They close on the nearest rival and would rather hit from the side that throws them into an edge, a drop, a hazard or the slag (they follow the flight a hit would give a dazed body: `knockedInto`, src/brain/tactics.ts), but a line-up is a preference, not a gate: with a rival in reach about a second (`press`, a trait: a Hard bot sooner, an Easy one slower) they take the hit they have, and at once when cornered or with the slag close. Empty-handed they fetch from a box within 14 tiles unless a rival they could shove into something is right there; they dodge only a hit that's coming and go straight back at them, get over a shove that goes nowhere (onto the rival's head and down the far side) instead of walking away, and climb from the slag only once it pours and comes close (before, they read the view's rounding as the slag moving and climbed from GO, a third of their time). Bots read a rival's build-up from its look (`RunnerSense.hurt`) and follow its flight that much further (`knockedInto`), so a hurt rival lines up from more places. Whoever has taken hits smoulders: a pale wisp off the head at one or two hits, a grey plume with sparks spitting off the body and hot feet at three or four, and from five a black plume, more sparks and the feet glowing (src/render/buildup.ts).

Bodies shove harder than in a race (`RunRules.shove`, `BRAWL_SHOVE` in src/game/modes.ts; DESIGN.md → Players, side contact), the same for everyone in the brawl: a walk moves someone standing there at half a walk (a race's quarter moves them 20 px before the walker slips through them), whoever is shoved off a ledge goes out over the drop at the speed they were shoved, and a run-up into someone who isn't pushing back is a body check: bonked like a bump from below, stars and credit, thrown on ahead a little faster than the runner came. Pushing back holds your ground against a walk, as in a race. Measured on 12 arenas, from every ground stand cell the field reaches outside the spawn pockets with a rival right beside it (2,550 tries, 1,608 with three tiles of floor for a run-up): a half-second walk into someone standing there put them off their ledge 24% of the time (8% with a race's shove), a run-up from three tiles back 72% (31%), and with the drop two to four tiles away 86–90% (5–7%); someone running straight back held against a walk (7%) but not a check (71%, the stars keeping their hands off the controls). On open floor a run-up carries someone standing there 7.1 tiles (5.9 fighting it), a walk's half-second lean 1.7 (a race's: 2.3 and 0.6, and 0.9). At three quarters of a walk, or a check from about 9 tiles/s, bots' rounds with parts placed ran 19 s (11 of 64 under 10 s) for no more deaths off the sides. Items already throw people off ledges and are as they were: a hit on someone standing still within two tiles of a drop sent them off it 61–89% of the time for the noodle, the bat, the Sucker Punch, the shell and the Stunnerang, 93–99% when they ran straight back (stars for 28 frames: a noodle carries someone standing still 10.8 tiles on open floor, 9.4 fighting it); a wall or a lip in the way (5% of tries) stopped nearly all of them (2–11% went over, 27–34% for the bat's full charge and the punch). Two items reach someone a swing can't (owner decision: a player hiding on a ledge must be reachable), dealt only by a brawl's boxes, twice each in its deal of 15: the Rude Awakening, an alarm clock lobbed over a lip or up onto a ledge that rings a beat and then throws every rival within three tiles out from it and up (a tile off, 9.0 tiles along open floor standing, 7.6 running back; 5.5 at two tiles), its thrower spared; and the Catch of the Day, a rod whose line flies straight out about ten tiles (or slanting up, through boards) and yanks the first rival it snags back toward the caster (2.2, 3.7 and 7.4 tiles from four, six and ten tiles off, ending short of the caster's feet), the one item that pulls rather than pushes. On the 12 arenas, from every ground stand cell two tiles or less from a drop, a blast from a tile on the far side sent them off 78% of the time (100% running back; the noodle 80% and 99% on the same cells), and a straight cast from across the drop 87% (97%); the slanting cast reaches only someone near the lip of rock overhead (48 of 174 tries) but anyone on a board. Bots cast at a rival the line's sketch reaches and lob where the arc's sketch comes down within two tiles of a rival standing on something, the one it would throw into something first: 73% of their casts snag someone and 28% of their lobs throw someone (48 bare rounds). With them dealt, four Normal bots on the bare arena ran 17.2 s a round from 16.8, 73% of deaths knocked in both, 0% draws, 25% of the time somewhere safe from 27%; with parts placed 18.3 s from 17.6, 70% knocked from 66%, 5% draws from 2%, and 18.4 uses a minute of fighting from 19.2 (two-use items that wait for a shot). Over 96 more bare seeds the knocked share held (65% against 68%), so no shift beyond noise.

## Gauntlet (planned): the co-op puzzle platformer (PICO PARK)

What we'd borrow: everyone has to reach the goal; levels scale to the number of players; failure is shared.

| Attribute | Reference | Target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Players | 2–8 (10 in Classic) [PP-steam] | 2–8 |
| Clear | the key opens the door, and every player must reach it [PP-steam] | every runner reaches the flag |
| Scaling | each level adjusts its terrain to the player count [PP-site] | the crate and the level's asks scale with players |
| Failure | one death and everyone restarts (a review; not official) [PP-review] | one death and the team's run ends; the round scores what the team got |
| Scoring | no per-level scores documented; a shared high score in Endless [PP-wiki] | rounds cleared in a row, against a target |
| Parts | n/a | the crate deals hazards; the team builds the level against itself |

What it needs from us: Race's "everyone finished scores nothing" flipped, team scoring, and a crate mix (`ModeDef.crate`).
