# src/render: presentation only

- Read sim state, never write it. Render-side state (particles, verlet rigs, caches) must never influence the sim. `Math.random` is fine here: it's cosmetic.
- `Renderer.render` (`renderer.ts`) paints world-space Canvas2D layers through the camera: **background**, **foreground** (transparent; the world pass draws to it through `rc.s`), **emissive** (additive), **light map** (0.5 = neutral). `post.ts` composites them through the cel pass (`cel-shader.ts`: light bands, ink, hard shadows) in WebGL2, with a Canvas2D fallback that has none of that. The HUD (`hud.ts`) is its own canvas.
- Everything on `rc.s` gets ink along its edges. Thin or soft things (grass, smoke, dust, motes, afterimages) turn into black scrub or solid discs there, so draw them on the background layer, `rc.bg`.
- Everything draws in logical view coordinates, and the window is `ViewFrame` (`frame.ts`): the VIEW_W×VIEW_H play view sits inside it at a fixed aspect, and a layer's job is to fill the frame, not the view. Full-screen fills use the frame (`Hud.wash`). The camera's only inputs are `setBounds` (world size, from the level) and `setFrame`. Phones drive it from `camera-follow.ts`, which holds the close follow and the hand-moved pick/place view; `Camera` knows nothing about either. The rule that `frameAll` wins with more than one player in the match lives in `PhoneCam.frameRace`.
- Past the world's edge is `surround.ts`: scenery built from the level, painted by `TerrainPainter`. Past the left and right edges it draws nothing (they're open air and leaving is death, so out there must read as a drop, never as ground); ceilings carry on up. Below the world, bottom-row columns just run on down and are drawn through the pool (`drawSunken`), except where the ground floats (`FLOATS`: space). Scenery never draws inside the level past its ring (`Surround.draw` clips it out, and `tests/view-frame.test.ts` checks the tiles), and a bake chunk with no ground in it never touches an ink layer.
- Runners are the cast (`docs/CHARACTERS.md`): `ink-character.ts` is the frame every kind shares (pose, squash, bend, gait, arms, face, shadow), `ink-figure.ts` the pen and face kit, and each kind is a `KindArt` in `KINDS` (`cast/kinds.ts`): a hand-inked outline, a signature part and a gait. A kind with frames (`cast/sprites/<kind>/frames.ts`, made by `tools/art3d/README.md`'s pipeline, registered in `LOADERS`) is drawn from them, and its `KindArt` only until they load. Keep the pen light and in a dark of the body's color, and the player's color the loudest note.
- Prop art is a registry: one file per module type in `props/modules/<type>.ts` registering `ModuleArt` (`props/common.ts`), imported from `props/modules/index.ts`. A type may register several arts on different layers. Don't add per-type `switch`es elsewhere.
- A crate machine (launcher, jet, laser, zapper, crusher, fan, magnet) draws its whole cell as the machine, and its body block isn't drawn under it (`housed` in `props/machine.ts`). Its outline is the machine's shape (a cannon on its carriage, a turret on a tripod), never the tile's square: it stays inside the cell without filling it. Draw it in the local frame `tf` turns to the way it faces, so the mount stays at the back. A launcher that takes two tiles (`LAUNCHER_FRAMES` in `sim/props/launcher-frames.ts`) is a `frame` look in `props/modules/launcher-framed.ts`: its pieces are drawn round the middle of its cells and turned with the part (a half turn mirrors), its outline is the sub-tile `shape` the sim stands it as, read from the same numbers, and its `pivot` is the sim's `at` (`launcherPivot`, checked by `tests/launcher-frames.test.ts`).
- Art on the `BACKDROP` layer paints before the terrain, which hides it: use it for the scenery half of a set piece (towers, masts, gantries), never for anything a player can touch or read a warning from. `tests/render-layers.test.ts` holds the list of module types allowed to use it. A landmark's shell shows through the gaps in its walls, so give it a colour of its own (the barn's back wall is raw boards, not the roof's red).
- Fair play (docs/ART.md): `pnpm shot fairplay [--theme T] [--concept id]` races every landmark and set piece, reads the play layer back and fails on anything opaque and thicker than a brace that lies past the sim's shapes by more than `EDGE_DRIFT`, and on a landmark shell that, in the finished picture, shows the colour of its walls through a notch or slot in them. Known lies waiting on a call are its `PENDING` and `PENDING_SHELLS` lists (`--strict` shows them); shorten them, don't grow them.
- Shared drawing helpers and lights are in `kit.ts`, and the pen is in `ink.ts`; reuse them before writing new ones. Prop, item and set-piece art uses the kit in `ink-props.ts`: hand-cut shapes (`boxPts`, `ovalPts`, `poly`), two flat tones (`tone`), the pen (`pen`, `part`), and `sprite`/`stamp`, which bake a still piece once and stamp it every frame. Bake what holds still; draw live only what moves, glows or is a tell. Big scratch canvases come from `kit.scratch`, never a fresh canvas per call. Clear a reused canvas with `wipe` (kit), never one whole-canvas `clearRect`: with nothing drawn after it, Chrome can keep the old picture, and `pnpm shot themeswap` catches a bake that leaks into the next level.
- A theme's backdrop shells take their fog haze and pen from `hazer(d, SHELL)` and draw flat shapes with `flat` (`props/skins/common.ts`); the theme's `HazeSpec` holds its own numbers. Decor art reads its seed through `decor-kit.ts` (`bits01` or a theme's `hashKnobs`, and `low16`). `slag/no-copied-kit` (lint) stops a file redefining these.
- Glacier and space reach their set pieces through looks other themes own; `props/skins/<theme>.ts` (one `ThemeSkins` per theme: `rotor`, `cannon`, `deco`, collected in `props/skins/index.ts`) gives a theme its own art for a look or a deco kind. Add a skin to the theme's own file rather than branching on the theme in a module.
- Weather (`weather.ts`): `WEATHER_ART` is the registry. What's in the air is a stateless field drawn on `rc.bg`, what's on the ground is baked by the terrain painter through `coat`, and mist is one small canvas the cel pass cuts into bands over everything. Anything you see of weather does something: nothing in a theme falls or blows on its own, and anything that sways goes through `flutter` (`kit.ts`), which is exactly 0 in still air. Read gusts through `gustShown` (`RC.gust`) so a warning you see is the one the sim is running.
- Art never casts rays. Read how far a beam, column or lane reaches with `beamOf` (`props/common.ts`): the sim hurts with that same number, so what you draw stops where it stops hurting.
- Glow is for hot things only (fire, molten metal, lasers, sparks, muzzle flashes): `glowDot` on `rc.g`. A lens, LED, gem or warning lamp is a flat `bulb` (kit), and a telegraph is a flat bright shape, never a soft halo. Bloom only lifts glow above `BLOOM_KNEE` (`cel-shader.ts`). A point light (`light`) is for things that throw light; a runner carries only a small neutral key light, because a colored pool tints the pack into one hue. A rendered figure takes no light band itself (`markFigures`, `figAmbient` in `cel-shader.ts`): bands are for the world.
- Keep per-frame allocation low: the game targets 60 fps with up to 8 players and ~100 lights.

Verify every visual change by looking at it: `pnpm shot race --race all`, `pnpm shot levels --themes all`, `pnpm shot props`, or the scenario closest to your change, then open the printed PNG. The harness fails on page errors.

## Ink

World art follows the ink rules in `docs/ART.md`. `ink.ts` is the pen and the outline tracer; `ink-terrain.ts` composes terrain and holds `INK_CAPS` and `INK_PLATFORMS`; `ink-material.ts` holds `INK_MATERIALS`; `ink-parts.ts` holds part art. A new `TerrainKind`, `CapKind` or `PlatformKind` is a registry entry, and `tsc` lists what's missing.

- **Every mark is keyed to the world.** Its place, seed and length come from world position on a fixed lattice, never from walk order, an index along a run, or where a run started. Long edges lift at fixed cells (`cellStrokes`), because a stroke's pressure runs from its start. Break this and a bomb changes things it never touched.
- Cut a bake to a shape with `cutOutside` (`kit.ts`), never a `destination-in` fill: Chrome's GPU raster can bound that op and leave what lies outside the path, flat ground floating in the sky.
- A bomb redraws only `dirtyTiles`: the changed tiles plus `margin`, the furthest one tile can reach. A mark that reads further than that (a material deciding by depth, say) has to stay inside it or the margin has to grow. Every painter gets an `Area`: skip what can't reach it, and the clip does the rest.
- A stage concept's landmark draws its tiles in `Level.landmark.skin` (`skinOf` in `ink-terrain.ts`, looks in `SKIN_LOOKS`): the skin's material, cap and boards inside its box, a footing where it stands on the theme's ground, and none of the theme's grass, hangers, veins or decor on it. Its backdrop shells are deco kinds in `props/modules/deco-shells.ts`. Check skins with `pnpm shot landmarks` and `pnpm shot inkblast --skin K`.
- Run `pnpm shot inkblast --seed S --theme T` after changing terrain art. It bites craters out of a level and compares each partial redraw against a full bake. A large `wrong` means a mark isn't keyed to the world: a few faint pixels are antialiasing where strokes overlap.
- `PW_GPU=1` runs `pnpm shot` and `pnpm perf` on the machine's real GPU, still headless. `pnpm shot inkdraw` times the cel pass alone, and `pnpm perf --cpu 4` slows the main thread to stand in for a phone.

## Drawing a landmark, hazard or decor piece

Theme art shares the screen with the terrain, the crate parts and the runners, so it has to look drawn by the same hand: a light pen in a dark of each colour, two flat tones and a few marks. The rules are docs/ART.md → World art; this is how to meet them with the kit (`ink-props.ts`, `ink.ts`, `kit.ts`).

| Do                                                                                                                                                           | Don't                                                                                      |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| A body is `part(g, boxPts(…) / ovalPts(…) / poly(…), base, seed, { depth, thin: 0.4–0.6, thick: 1–1.4 })`: hand-cut shape, two tones, pen in `inkFor(base)`. | `fillRect` or `rect` for a body, `tone` with no pen, or a pen in `INK`.                    |
| A post, spar, rope, pipe or strut is `barLine(g, ax, ay, bx, by, w, colour, seed, { lit: 14 })` or `bar(g, pts, …)`: rim 0.3 a side in a dark of it.         | `g.fillStyle = INK; line(… w + 1 …)` and the colour over it: a black marker rim.           |
| Marks are `tick(g, …, 0.3–0.6, seed, css(surface, -30))`, at most three a tile, lifted and uneven.                                                           | A ruled grid: planks, lattice or clapboard every few px at one width with `taper: [0, 0]`. |
| A rivet or hole is `dot(g, x, y, r, seed, fleck, inkFor(plate))`; a hole's fill is `css(surface, -35)`.                                                      | `'#111'`, `'#120c08'` or any hard-coded near-black.                                        |
| A lamp, lens or gem is a flat `bulb`; only fire, lava and sparks glow (`glowDot` on `rc.g`); an arc is `zap` on `rc.g`.                                      | Gradients, soft halos on cold things, raw `ctx.stroke()`.                                  |
| Solid things on `rc.s`; smoke, steam, dust and anything thin or translucent on `rc.bg`.                                                                      | Washes and faint strokes on `rc.s`: the cel pass inks their edges into black scrub.        |
| Colour from the theme or the piece's style (`d.pal`, `th.terrain`, `d.style`), calm enough to survive the cel pass's chroma knee.                            | Saturated primaries on big areas: a blue cart box next to a brown field.                   |
| Let the cel pass cast the shadow; `tone` gives the shape its own shade.                                                                                      | A painted drop shadow under the piece: it doubles the cel shadow.                          |
| A big flat surface (a hull, a wall, a sail) gets a second tone cut as a shape and two or three marks.                                                        | A big flat fill with one colour and nothing on it, or one with a mark every tile.          |

Sizes: the pen's `thin`/`thick` are relative weights (× `PEN.weight`, 0.62), so 0.45/1.1 draws 0.28–0.68 world px. A bar keeps its width: a rope 0.8–1.2, a post 1.6–3. A mark is lighter than the contour round it.

```ts
/** A milk churn: a hand-cut tin body, a lid, a handle bar and two marks. Baked once, stamped. */
function churn(g: CanvasRenderingContext2D, x: number, y: number, seed: number) {
  const tin: HSL = hsl3(205, 8, 66);
  part(g, poly(x - 3.4, y, x - 3.6, y - 7, x - 2, y - 9, x + 2, y - 9, x + 3.6, y - 7, x + 3.4, y), tin, seed, { depth: 0.8, thin: 0.4, thick: 1 });
  part(g, boxPts(x - 2.4, y - 10.6, 4.8, 1.8, 0.6, seed + 1), tin, seed + 1, { depth: 0.3, thin: 0.35, thick: 0.8 });
  barLine(g, x - 2.2, y - 6.4, x + 2.2, y - 6.4, 0.7, hsl3(205, 6, 48), seed + 2);
  tick(g, x - 3.2, y - 3, x + 3.2, y - 3.1, 0.4, seed + 3, css(tin, -28));
  dot(g, x + 1.6, y - 4.4, 0.4, seed + 4, undefined, inkFor(tin));
}
```

Check it: `pnpm shot artqa --theme T` (`--only landmarks|hazards|decor`) shoots every landmark, hazard and decor piece of a theme mid-race beside its terrain and three parts, at play scale and 2×; open the crops and compare the pen, the tones and the marks with the ground and the parts in the same frame. `tests/ink-rules.test.ts` fails on the mechanical faults (raw strokes, `INK`, a pen helper left on its near-black default, hard-coded near-blacks, gradients) in theme art and crate-part art (every module art file, `ink-parts.ts`, `items*.ts`), and every new `decor-<theme>.ts`, skin or module art file is held to it by name.
