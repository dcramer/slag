# src/brain: bots

A brain is an input device (docs/DESIGN.md → AI players): it fills a seat and produces that seat's `InputFrame`s, and nothing else crosses into the match. Only the machine that runs a bot runs its brain; every other peer just sees its inputs, so nothing here has to be deterministic across browsers. Built-in brains still take a seed and draw from `Rng`, so a headless trial replays exactly.

- **Senses** (`view.ts`, `types.ts`): `BrainView` is versioned (`BRAIN_VIEW_VERSION`) and rounded on purpose: positions in quarter tiles, speeds as words, parts as the cells they hold (`barrierOf`), never names or sim objects; items, which a player reads by their look, by kind (held, loose, and the mystery boxes that pop them). The race's `objective` is the mode's. A brain reads only the view. Bump the version when its shape changes.
- **Mind** (`brains/`, listed in `BRAINS` in `registry.ts`): `think(view)` returns an `Intent` (`ready`, `hover`, `pick`, `place`, `aim`, `goTo`, `leap`, `hop`, `use`, `giveUp`, `wait`). A new brain is a `BrainDef` there.
- **Strategies** (`strategies/`, `STRATEGIES` keyed by `ModeId`): how a bot races in each mode, made fresh each race. The mode gives the view its `objective` (`OBJECTIVES` in `view.ts`: reach a cell, or climb to a loop's lap line); `Headway` (`strategies/headway.ts`) says when it's stuck, to leap or give up; `ItemPlay` (`strategies/items.ts`) uses the held item and fetches boxes and loose items, the same for every mode. Pick and place are the brain's own and shared; a mode whose parts belong somewhere other than the way to a flag says where with its strategy's `lay` (Brawl: where a knock throws someone, `knockLanes`, away from where the field starts), which placing reads in place of the route. A new mode fails typecheck in both registries until it has an entry; never branch on the mode inside a brain.
- **Item tactics** (`tactics.ts`, `ITEM_TACTICS` keyed by `ItemKind`): when each held item is worth pressing use, from the view alone (`Moment`: the view, the bot, whether it's stalled). A new item is one entry there; typecheck asks for it. Whether a bot sees its moment is its `knack` trait; a test or a stats suite measuring the game without item use sets it to 0.
- **Motor** (`motor.ts`): turns intents into buttons. It may read the real match, as a player's hands act on the real game, but it only presses buttons: cursors move with the arrows and click, and a race plays the controller's own recorded moves (`movesFrom` in `src/sim/reach.ts`) toward the route's distance field (`route.ts`, which reads the race the way the solvability proof does, `fieldOf`). Moves are traced in the parts' winds as they will blow (`RaceMap.winds`). It drops a move that meets a hazard on the parts' cycles, a shot in flight, a part's solid moving into its way, a part a runner set off as it will go (`foresight.ts`: one already shaking from its live state, one the move itself sets off from the zone its `sense` watches), or (in the air) a gust changing under it; looks one move past a landing a hazard is about to reach (with a walk-speed way onto each cell beside the quickest, for ice a run can't stop on; past every clear move while where it stands is about to hurt); weighs a wait by the time it costs; and picks again whenever the body leaves its recording. A move of its own making (a hop under a box, a leap of faith) is played out first (`playFrom` in `src/sim/reach.ts`) and held to the same checks: one that leaves the map, drops into the pit or meets a hazard it doesn't take. At its target it lets go only where coasting leaves it standing, and brakes otherwise; on a ledge's lip with its middle over the drop it steps in first, since a brain reads its cell by its middle. A strategy that fights rather than runs (`StrategyDef.haste`, Brawl) sends its `goTo`s with `haste`: the motor drops the move under way once the target moves (in the air at once, on the ground once its reaction has passed or at once if it's running away), takes no reaction for a rival's shove and only what's left of it after a landing it read in the air, and lines up in a cell only where `linesUp` says that wins the time back, walking there rather than tapping. A run keeps all of that as it was, so its levels and its bot levels play as they did: never branch on the mode in the motor, say it through the intent. Measure a change to it with Hard bots over every theme and size with parts placed (`tests/lib/hard-routes.ts`, `botRace` in `tests/lib/bot-race.ts` takes `theme`, `size` and `parts`), and look at a miss frame by frame before fixing it: most are a position it walked into where every later move is hit.
- **Personality** (`personality.ts`): a bot's level (`Player.bot`, Easy/Normal/Hard, set on its card through the `bot` action) gives the ranges; its seed rolls where in them it sits. The motor reads the hands (reaction, slip, choke, save, think, muse, stray, care, margin, hurry), the brain the pace, spite, block, weigh, knack and press (how hard it presses a fight: how soon it takes an unlined hit, when it shoves rather than fetch, how soon it tries another way past a stalled shove; temperaments tilt it). No level is flawless: a fumble is one of the slips a level's difficulty counts (`slip` in `src/gen/level/precision.ts`), and on a tight jump (`fatalSlips`) it's one that drops it. New behavior that should differ between bots goes in as a trait here, not as a branch on the level. Measure the motor itself with `steady` hands (`botRace(..., { steady: true })`), so a bot's lapses don't hide whether its moves and the route agree.
- **Temperament and grudges** (`temperament.ts`, `grudge.ts`): what a bot wants, rolled from its seed apart from its level, and never shown. A temperament is data in `TEMPERAMENTS`: heat per slight (`kill`, `route`, `lead`, `snatch`), how long it remembers, mischief, and a tilt to its hands. The brain keeps the ledger from the view alone (kills, parts' owners, scores, who took a card) and the trails runners left last race; its `spite` trait decides how often it acts and how well. A new temperament is an entry there, not a branch in the brain.
- **Blocking** (`brains/runner.ts`): with the way through in hand (`covered`), a bot whose `block` roll comes up puts a hazard or a block on `view.route()` and weighs up to `weigh` spots with `view.assess`, a spot every few frames, staggered by seat. `assess` and `keepsWay` only keep the plan everyone is building (never search for another), and ask through the level's shared caches (`cacheFor` for reach, `JumpCache` for precision), so a spot re-traces only what it changes. A question about a level in play goes through those caches, never a cold `analyzeRoutes` or `prove`'s search: a cold one is tens of milliseconds inside a fixed step. A spot the brain places on must be one the cursor can aim at (`canPlace` says so), and it gives up a spot it can't get placed and lets go of the controls in the end, so an untimed place phase never waits on a bot forever.
- **Seats** (`bots.ts`, `src/game/device.ts`): a bot is a local device of its machine (`bot1`, online `<peer>:bot1`), named so the match can tell (an all-bot lobby starts itself), seated with a `join` action; `BotSeats.frames` is merged with the keyboards' before `net.tick`. Only the lobby's seated host (or an offline game) adds bots. The game gives its bots a wall-clock budget per frame (`BotBudget`, `BOT_THINK_MS` in `src/game-client.ts`, shared by a frame's catch-up steps): bots take turns going first, and past the budget the rest keep their last intent and the motor plays on with the move under way without picking a new one (`Motor.drive` `rushed`). Online the host runs every bot, so a bot that stalls the host stalls every peer. Headless trials pass no budget and replay exactly.

Nothing in `sim/`, `gen/` or `game/` may import from here (lint enforces it). Check a change with `pnpm vitest run tests/brain.test.ts`; when you change the motor, the route, a strategy or a personality range, run `pnpm test:stats bot-finish bot-hard-routes slag-climb` and compare the rates with the bands in `src/game/expectations.ts` and `tests/lib/hard-routes.ts`; for item tactics, `pnpm vitest run tests/brain-items.test.ts` and `pnpm test:stats slag-catch-up`; for the brawl strategy or a knock tactic (`knockedInto`, which follows a dazed body's flight into whatever kills it), `pnpm test:stats brawl` against `BRAWL_ROUNDS`, whose fight bands (first bonk, items used, still, retreating, distance) fail a bot that turtles. A strategy reading the slag compares rows it has seen, never one frame's change: the view rounds its feet and the slag apart, so a jump reads as the slag moving.
